Scope: product facts about Microsoft Dynamics 365 Business Central (BC) that Verdana's Swedish website can state, each with an official source. This file says nothing about Verdana's own partner status or prices. None of that is verified, and nothing here should be read as implying it.

How to read the findings:
- Every finding was checked on **2026-10-11**. Where Microsoft shows a page date (`ms.date`/updated), it is quoted to show how fresh the page is.
- Confidence levels:
  - **High**: stated explicitly on an official Microsoft page or document that was read on the check date.
  - **Medium**: official but indirect. This covers a Microsoft document hosted by a third party, a Microsoft community-manager forum reply, official sources that disagree, a date converted from Microsoft's raw timestamp, or a point read from an automated page summary.
  - **Low**: secondary (non-Microsoft) source only. These are marked "(secondary)".
- Recent changes to flag in site copy:
  - Cloud list price increase on 2025-11-01, the first in more than five years.
  - Sales Order Agent and Payables Agent generally available since November 2025.
  - Expense Agent in paid public preview since June 2026.
  - Release plans replaced by the "AI at Work roadmap" from September 2026.
  - Version 29.0 (2026 release wave 2) shipped in October 2026. In it, "Microsoft Copilot" replaces "Chat with Copilot" and is a preview.
  - Dynamics NAV 2016 left extended support in April 2026. NAV 2017 leaves it in January 2027.
  - BC on-premises version 26 support ends on 2026-10-13 PT (Microsoft lists 10/14/2026 06:59:59).

## 1. Official positioning: how Microsoft describes Business Central (Swedish and English)

### Takeaway
Microsoft positions Business Central as an AI-powered ERP ("affärssystem (ERP)") for small and midsize businesses, "trusted by 55,000 companies". It connects finance, sales, service and operations in one solution, with Microsoft Copilot included in Essentials and AI agents for everyday finance and operations work. Microsoft's public product page talks only about the cloud. It does not mention on-premises deployment.

## 2. Licensing and price: editions, list prices (SEK/USD/EUR), last change, commitment, minimum rules, trial, capacity add-ons

### Takeaway
Business Central online is sold per user through CSP:
- **Essentials:** $80 per user/month.
- **Premium:** $110 per user/month.
- **Team Members:** $8 per user/month.
- **Device:** $45 per device/month.
- **External Accountant:** up to 3 per tenant.
- **Microsoft 365 users:** free read-only access in Teams.

US prices are paid yearly and have applied since **2025-11-01**, when Microsoft raised BC prices for the first time in more than five years. Essentials went from $70 to $80, Premium from $100 to $110 and Device from $40 to $45, and the storage entitlement per license rose at the same time.

Microsoft's Swedish pricing page shows **764,70 kr / 1 051,40 kr / 76,50 kr** per user/month, "betalas årsvis", "Pris exklusive moms". Microsoft labels these prices informational: the actual price is set at checkout or by the CSP reseller.

Other terms:
- The free trial is 30 days and can be extended to up to 90 days of trial experience.
- Each tenant gets 80 GB of database storage, plus a per-license allowance, 1 production environment and 3 sandbox environments. Add-ons cover extra storage and environments.
- AI agents need Copilot Credits, which are sold separately.

## 3. Deployment: cloud (SaaS) versus on-premises, Microsoft's direction, and whether new on-premises licenses are sold

### Takeaway
BC runs online (SaaS, Microsoft-hosted on Azure; Swedish environments are hosted in Azure geography "Sweden") or on-premises (self-, partner- or IaaS-hosted). Microsoft's product messaging, AI features, Teams access with Microsoft 365 licenses and several Power BI features are online-only or online-first.

On-premises licenses are still sold through the Dynamics Price List, under a Perpetual or a Subscription model (on-premises licensing guide, November 2025). A Microsoft community manager confirmed that an announced end date for perpetual on-premises purchases applies to **new customers** only. Partner sources date this end to 2025-04-01. Cloud subscriptions also carry non-perpetual dual use rights for on-premises deployment.

New BC on-premises versions still ship, including a Swedish (SE) package for version 29.

### Cited Findings
- The on-premises licensing guide (November 2025) says:
  - "Business Central, on-premises licenses are only available through the Dynamics Price List (DPL) and licensed through one of two licensing models: Perpetual Licensing, Subscription Licensing".
  - Subscription licenses run as "full month terms that may be cancelled at the end of the term without penalties".
  - Under Subscription, the solution is partner-hosted SaaS, or deployed on the customer's premises only if "their Business Central, on-premises partner provides day-to-day management services and maintains sole control over the solution". A self-managed on-premises deployment is shown as Perpetual-only.

## 5. Migration: official paths from Dynamics NAV, GP and other systems to Business Central online

### Takeaway
Microsoft's cloud migration tool runs on Azure Data Factory with an Integration Runtime. It supports these sources:
- **BC on-premises v25 and later:** migrate directly.
- **BC on-premises v15–24:** upgrade to at least v25 first.
- **BC on-premises v14:** either a full upgrade or the "BC 14 reimplementation tool", which moves essential data only.
- **Dynamics NAV:** must first be upgraded to BC on-premises. There is no direct NAV-to-cloud path.
- **Dynamics GP 2015 and later.**
- **Dynamics SL 2015 and later.**
- **Any other SQL source**, via custom migrations. "Migrate to the cloud from any SQL database" became GA in v28.
- **QuickBooks**, via Microsoft apps.

C/AL customizations must be converted to AL extensions. Otherwise data from customized tables isn't carried forward. The source must run SQL Server 2016 or later at compatibility level 130 or higher.

## 9. Microsoft partner program terms: what a firm without certifications must not claim

### Takeaway
To resell or service BC online licenses, a company needs a Microsoft partner ID (Microsoft AI Cloud Partner Program) and **CSP enrollment**, either as a direct-bill partner or as an indirect reseller through a distributor. BC licenses are sold only through CSP. The reselling partner is the customer's first line of support and must hold Advanced Support for Partners or Premier to escalate to Microsoft.

**"Solutions Partner for Business Applications"** is a designation earned with a partner capability score of at least 70/100. The score has five metrics: net customer adds, intermediate certifications such as the BC Functional Consultant, advanced certifications such as the BC Developer, usage growth and deployments. Each needs at least one point.

**"Small and Midsize Business Management"** is a Business Applications specialization. It requires that designation plus extra performance and skilling criteria.

Verdana should not claim any designation, specialization, "certifierad partner" status, CSP status or Microsoft endorsement unless Partner Center shows it.
